Chapter 12: MRP-VM

MRP-VM (Meta-Rational Pragmatic Virtual Machine) is the execution model that turns natural-language-derived tasks into executable, controlled programs with interpreters, policies, traces, validation, and persistent state. It is the runtime that makes AssistOS agents not just conversational interfaces, but verifiable executors.

The Core Idea

Today's AI agents typically work by passing natural language back and forth between an LLM and a set of tools. This is flexible but unverifiable — you cannot easily audit what the agent decided, why it made a decision, or whether it followed constraints. MRP-VM changes this by treating natural-language tasks as executable programs with explicit structure.

An MRP-VM program has: an interpreter selected by task type, a policy that constrains execution, a trace of what happened, a validator that checks correctness, and persistent state that survives across interactions. The result is not just an answer — it is a verifiable execution record.

Interpreters and Task Types

MRP-VM does not use a single reasoning engine for all tasks. Instead, it selects specialized interpreters based on the nature of the problem:

  • Research interpreters — for literature analysis, hypothesis generation, experiment design, and data exploration. NeuroVSA is one such interpreter, specialized in Vector Symbolic Architectures.
  • Analysis interpreters — for statistical analysis, machine learning, genomic sequence processing, and structured data exploration.
  • Legal interpreters — for document analysis, clause comparison, obligation extraction, and compliance checking.
  • Security interpreters — for event stream analysis, anomaly detection, policy violation detection, and threat assessment.
  • Workflow interpreters — for scientific protocol execution, data pipeline orchestration, and multi-step analysis.

Each interpreter has its own representational machinery, validation methods, and knowledge accumulation patterns. The LLM provides linguistic flexibility and hypothesis generation; the interpreter provides the actual reasoning substrate.

Policies and Constraints

Every MRP-VM execution runs under a policy that defines what the interpreter can do, what data it can access, what outputs it can produce, and what validation steps are required. Policies can enforce:

  • Data access restrictions — what the interpreter can read and what is off-limits.
  • Output constraints — what formats, what level of detail, what must be excluded.
  • Validation requirements — what checks must pass before a result is accepted.
  • Audit requirements — what must be logged and what provenance must be recorded.
  • Human review gates — what requires human approval before proceeding.

Policies are not suggestions — they are enforced by the MRP-VM runtime. An interpreter cannot bypass policy; it can only work within its constraints or refuse execution.

Traces and Validation

Every MRP-VM execution produces a trace: what inputs were used, what steps were taken, what decisions were made, what validation was applied, and what result was produced. This trace is not optional — it is the primary artifact of execution.

Validation happens at multiple levels:

  • Structural validation — does the output match the expected format and schema?
  • Constraint validation — were all policy constraints respected?
  • Semantic validation — does the result make sense given the inputs and the task?
  • Reproducibility validation — can the execution be repeated with the same inputs and produce the same result?

Results that fail validation are not silently accepted — they are flagged, explained, and either re-executed or escalated for human review.

Knowledge Accumulation

MRP-VM interpreters accumulate knowledge through Knowledge Units — auditable units of experimental knowledge that capture what worked, what failed, what conditions apply, and what the confidence level is. KUs are not just notes; they are structured, versioned, and linked to specific problem classes, representations, and validation outcomes.

Over time, an interpreter becomes more effective not by vague adaptation, but by accumulating validated evidence and converting it into operational selection policies. This is how NeuroVSA learns which VSA representation works for which problem class, and how the Trustworthy AI Agent learns which verification methods are most effective for which model types.

Relation to AGISystem2

MRP-VM's validation capabilities are underpinned by the AGISystem2 reasoning framework, which provides formal reasoning and knowledge management. While MRP-VM handles the execution model — interpreters, policies, traces, and state — AGISystem2 provides the logical validation layer that ensures results are not just plausible, but formally sound.

Vision

MRP-VM represents a shift from conversational AI to executable AI. Instead of agents that talk about what they might do, MRP-VM produces agents that do things in a verifiable, auditable, reproducible way. Natural language becomes the interface; structured execution becomes the reality. This is what makes AssistOS agents not just intelligent, but trustworthy.
