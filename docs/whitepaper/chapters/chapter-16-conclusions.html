Chapter 16: Next steps

The product test is an ordinary working day. Can a person give a Robot a bounded task, inspect its progress, step in when needed and keep a useful result in the project? The architecture should make that experience repeatable.

The foundation

Explorer and Ploinky provide the workspace and capability runtime. The documentation also describes WebMeet collaboration, AGISystem2 reasoning experiments and AxiFace visual identities. Individual repositories are the place to check supported behavior and setup.

A working component does not imply that every integration or end-to-end workflow in this whitepaper is complete.

Development and research

Wormhole, LightDSU and the DPU Agent address communication and data controls. ModelAutoResearcher, the Trustworthy AI Agent and Security Guardian describe broader research, evaluation and monitoring workflows.

MRP-VM investigates structured task execution. NeuroVSA explores representation choice through experiments. AKU organizes local knowledge. The federated learning architecture describes training across organizations with local participation and export decisions.

These projects have different levels of maturity. Their claims need implementation tests, evaluations and documentation tied to a specific version.

What to prove next

  1. Make one user productive with a few Robots on one machine.
  2. Make background, browser and computer-use tasks visible and interruptible.
  3. Keep team workspaces, project files and WebMeet rooms coherent and separate.
  4. Validate identity, access, budgets, failure reporting and recovery.
  5. Expand the capability catalog as supported workflows earn adoption.

Reviewable artifacts matter more than the appearance of autonomy. A task that stops at a sensible human decision may be more useful than one that proceeds without enough context.

Work with us

Developers can help package capabilities, test installation and report failures. Researchers can contribute reproducible experiments and comparisons. Teams can bring a recurring workflow with known inputs, stopping conditions and acceptance criteria.

A useful pilot measures human effort, completion rate, required corrections, intervention frequency and execution cost. Those results tell us what to improve and what should remain human-led.
